01

The problem

Cremorne's offices compete with the spare room at home. To bring people back, the street experience matters: shade, quiet, safe walking routes.

But most of the precinct is data dark. The council does not know where people walk, or which streets are too hot to use.

02

What I built

Lumen turns office windows into privacy safe pedestrian counters. They count people, but never store images or identities.

A digital twin models where the shade falls through the day. Walkers get cooler, quieter routes, and the council gets street fixes ranked by walkers helped per dollar.

03

How it works

  1. Understand the briefResearch what Cremorne's businesses and council actually need.
  2. Count, never recordComputer vision counts pedestrians on the device, so no footage leaves the window.
  3. Model the shadeA digital twin with pvlib shows where the sun hits each street, hour by hour.
  4. Route people wellRecommend cooler, quieter walks between buildings.
  5. Make the business caseRank street improvements by walkers helped per dollar, so the council can act.
04

What I found

Our team placed in the top 5 of 15 finalist teams and won the SMEC Spot Prize.

Testing the approach on 304 simulated trips across 76 buildings showed how much route choice changes once shade and crowding are visible.

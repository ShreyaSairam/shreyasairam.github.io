LabBot

A hospital lab robot you can talk to. Tell it what to do in plain English, and watch it plan, replan and deliver.

01

The problem

Hospital labs move hundreds of samples a day between reception, centrifuges, analysers and fridges. Robots can do the carrying, but staff should not need to program them.

A nurse wants to say “take the urgent bloods first” and trust the robot to get it right, even when someone puts down a wet floor sign.

02

What I built

A simulated lab with six patient samples, three analysers, a centrifuge, a fridge and a charging dock. You type or speak a task, and LabBot shows what it understood before it moves.

It catches mistakes too. Send a blood count to the chemistry analyser and it explains why it is using the right one instead. Tap the floor to drop a wet floor sign, or turn on a nurse walking the corridor, and it replans on the spot.

03

How it works

  1. Words to tool callsThe command becomes structured calls like move(sample 4, Analyser A). Urgent samples are sorted first, and “the rest” means whatever has not been named.
  2. Check against lab rulesEach call is checked before it runs: the right analyser for the test, chemistry spun first, a warning if an urgent sample goes to the fridge.
  3. Plan the route with A*The lab is a grid. A* finds the shortest path, and you can switch on a view of every cell it explored.
  4. Replan when the world changesA sign on the route or a nurse in the way triggers a new plan from wherever the robot is.
  5. Look after the batteryBefore each trip it checks it can get there and back to the dock. If not, it charges first.
  6. Optional cloud LLMA small Cloudflare Worker can send the command to Gemini with a fixed function schema. The key stays on the server, and the page falls back to the on-device parser if the cloud is slow or wrong.
04

What I found

Testing broke it twice, which was the useful part.

First, a wet floor sign in the only doorway to the analyser room trapped the robot for good. I added a second door, and now the app refuses any sign that would cut a stop off from the robot.

Second, with a nurse in the corridor and signs to go around, the detours added up and the battery fell to 2% before it reached the dock. The safety margin was too thin, so I raised the reserve from 8% to 20%.

05

What I learned

Show the plan before acting.Listing what LabBot understood, with its corrections, makes it easy to trust and easy to catch a misunderstanding.
An LLM needs guard rails.The same rule checks run on the cloud output as on the local parser. The model suggests; the rules decide.
What I'd do nextMove from a grid to a real map with ROS, add several robots sharing the corridor, and test with lab staff.

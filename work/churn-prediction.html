Churn Prediction: Employees to Bank Customers

Who is about to leave, why, and what to do about it. First for employees, then for bank customers.

01

The problem

Bank customers rarely close their accounts. They drift. Savings move out, the salary stops arriving, and by the time the account is closed, the decision was made months ago.

Employees are the same. By the time someone resigns, it is too late to ask what would have kept them.

02

What I built

Two live tools that answer as you change the inputs.

Employees: change overtime, pay, satisfaction or years since promotion, and see the chance they leave, the top three reasons, and a suggested action. Five models are compared underneath.

Bank customers: drag a customer's balance, income and cash withdrawals, and see the chance their money leaves within 3 months, the likely reason (a big purchase, income stopping, or living off savings), and the response that fits.

03

How it works

  1. Shape the data in SQL1.06 million raw transactions from an open Czech bank dataset become one row per account per month.
  2. Define leavingAn account drifts when its next 3 months average under 70% of its last 6 months.
  3. Build 23 signalsBalance trend, income, share of cash withdrawals, loan payments and more.
  4. Train on the past, test on the futureLightGBM learns from 1994 to mid 1997 and is tested on late 1997 to 1998, just like real life.
  5. Guess the reason, then run it liveA second model names the likely reason. Both models are exported so they run in the browser instantly.
04

What I found

The bank model reached 0.89 AUC, against 0.86 for a simple logistic regression. Its top 10% of flags caught 53% of the accounts that drifted. It named the reason correctly 67% of the time, against 48% for always guessing the most common one.

For employees, logistic regression scored best (0.85 AUC), so it runs live because its reasons are easy to explain. Overtime was the strongest single driver, and clustering found groups whose leaving rates ranged from 7% to 28%.

05

What I learned

Not everyone can be talked out of leaving.Someone saving for a wedding will move their money whatever you say. So the tool suggests a response that fits the reason, not just 'call them'.
Test on later months, not random rows.Random splits flatter a model. Out of time testing gives honest numbers.
What I'd do nextThe employee data is IBM's fictional dataset. Real HR data would need careful privacy handling and fairness checks.
